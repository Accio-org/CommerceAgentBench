# UCP 600 + ISBP 821 — Examiner's Cheat Sheet

> Reference excerpt for examining documents presented under a documentary
> credit. Verbatim wording paraphrased; refer to the official ICC
> publications for the authoritative text. This file is provided so any
> examiner can apply the rules without prior memorization — what is being
> tested is **application**, not recall.

## UCP 600 — Selected Articles

### Article 14 — Standard for Examination of Documents

- (a) Banks must examine a presentation on the basis of the documents
  alone, to determine on the basis of the documents alone whether they
  appear on their face to constitute a complying presentation.
- (b) A maximum of **five banking days** following the day of presentation
  is allowed for the bank to determine if a presentation is complying.
- (c) Documents must be presented **not later than 21 calendar days after
  the date of shipment** as described in these rules, but in any event
  not later than the expiry date of the credit.
- (d) **Data in a document need not be identical to, but must not conflict
  with, data in that document, any other stipulated document, or the
  credit.**
- (e) **In documents other than the commercial invoice**, the description
  of the goods, services, or performance may be in general terms not
  conflicting with their description in the credit.
- (f) If a credit requires presentation of a document other than a
  transport document, insurance document, or commercial invoice without
  stipulating the data or content, banks will accept the document as
  presented if its data content appears to fulfil the function of the
  required document.

### Article 18 — Commercial Invoice

- (a)(iii) **The description of the goods, services or performance in a
  commercial invoice must correspond with that appearing in the credit.**
  (Note: "correspond with" is read alongside ISBP 821 Section C — invoice
  may include additional data not appearing to refer to a different
  nature / classification / category of goods.)

### Articles 19–25 — Transport Documents

- (Art 20) **Bill of lading** must indicate the name of the carrier and
  be signed by:
    - the carrier or a named agent for or on behalf of the carrier, or
    - the master or a named agent for or on behalf of the master.
  Any signature by an agent must indicate whether the agent has signed
  for or on behalf of the carrier or the master. **If an agent signs, the
  agent must be identified as agent and the name of the carrier or master
  must be identifiable elsewhere on the BL** (unless the credit
  expressly allows a freight forwarder's BL signed in that capacity).
- (Art 20(a)(ii)) "On board" notation must indicate the date on which the
  goods were shipped on board.
- "Late shipment" = on-board date is after the LC's **latest date of
  shipment** (field 44C).

### Article 28 — Insurance Document

- (f)(i) **Minimum coverage must be at least 110% of the CIF or CIP value
  of the goods.** If CIF/CIP not determinable from the documents, then
  110% of the amount claimed under the credit.
- (e) An insurance document indicating that the cover is effective from a
  date no later than the date of shipment is acceptable, even if the
  document is dated later than the date of shipment.
- (f)(ii) Currency of the insurance document must be the same as the
  currency of the credit.
- **(g)** **The insurance document must indicate that the risks covered
  conform to those required by the credit.** If the credit lists named
  Institute Clauses (e.g. ICC(A), War, Strikes), the policy must show
  cover under each of those clauses. Substituting one clause for another
  is a discrepancy; **omitting any required clause is a discrepancy**.

### Article 30 — Tolerance

- (a) "About" or "approximately" → tolerance ±10%.
- (b) **Tolerance ±5% in the quantity of the goods** is allowed, **provided
  the credit does not state the quantity in terms of packing units or
  individual items** and total drawings do not exceed the credit amount.
- (c) **Even when partial shipments are not allowed, a tolerance not to
  exceed 5% less than the amount of the credit is allowed**, provided
  that (i) the quantity of the goods if stated in the credit is shipped
  in full and (ii) any unit price if stated in the credit is not reduced.

### Article 16 — Discrepant Documents, Waiver and Notice

- An issuing bank determining that documents do not constitute a complying
  presentation may refuse to honour. A single notice of refusal must be
  given **no later than the close of the fifth banking day** following
  the day of presentation, stating each discrepancy.

## ISBP 821 — Selected Practice Notes

- **A19 / A20** — **The title of a document may differ from the title
  named in the credit, or may be untitled**, **provided** the data
  content of the document fulfils the function of the required document.
  Example: where the credit calls for a "Commercial Invoice", a document
  titled "INVOICE", "EXPORT INVOICE", "TAX INVOICE", "CUSTOMS INVOICE",
  or "FINAL INVOICE" is acceptable — except that a document titled
  "PROFORMA INVOICE" or "PROVISIONAL INVOICE" is **not** acceptable.
- **A23** — Typographical or spelling anomalies in a word or sentence
  that do not affect the meaning of the word or sentence (for example,
  "mashine" instead of "machine", **case differences such as upper-case
  vs title-case in a name**, transposed letters) are **not** to be
  considered as discrepancies, **provided the meaning is clear**. The
  same principle applies to **country-name variations referring to the
  same sovereign state** (e.g. "CHINA", "P.R.C.", "PEOPLE'S REPUBLIC OF
  CHINA"; "USA", "U.S.A.", "UNITED STATES OF AMERICA").
- **C5** — Data in the commercial invoice need not be a mirror image of
  the description in the credit, but should not conflict with that
  description or with data in other documents. Additional descriptive
  data is acceptable provided it does not appear to refer to a
  **different nature, classification, or category** of goods.
- **Goods description on invoice** — examples of **unacceptable** changes
  (the standard ISBP illustrations):
  - "imitation suede shoes" where credit says "suede shoes"
  - "second-hand hydraulic drilling rig" where credit says "hydraulic
    drilling rig"
  In each case the difference points to a different nature or
  classification of goods (material, condition, grade). A defining
  material specification stated in the credit (e.g. fabric type, alloy
  grade, weave) is part of the classification — not optional.
- **Weights, quantities, and totals across documents** — every total
  quantity, gross weight, net weight, or measurement that appears on
  more than one stipulated document must be identical (or reconcilable
  by an explicit packaging breakdown). A discrepancy between the
  packing list total and the bill of lading total is a data conflict
  under UCP 600 art 14(d).
- **Carton/package counts** — total number of packages stated on a
  packing list and on a bill of lading must match.
- **Late presentation** — UCP 14(c) is strict; a covering schedule dated
  more than 21 days after the on-board date of the BL (where the credit
  imposes the standard 21-day window) is a discrepancy even if the
  credit expiry has not yet been reached.
- **K17 / K18** — **An insurance document indicating that it provides
  cover greater than that required by the credit is acceptable.** Extra
  perils, extension clauses (e.g. Theft, Pilferage and Non-Delivery —
  "TPND"), or higher coverage percentages **are not discrepancies**.
  The bank only checks that the *minimum* required cover is met; any
  extra cover the beneficiary chose to buy is the beneficiary's own
  decision.
- **Required documents must be presented** — UCP 14(a) read alongside
  field 46A: every document listed as required in the credit must
  appear in the presentation. If field 46A lists, say, an Inspection
  Certificate or a Beneficiary's Certificate and the covering schedule
  does not enclose it, the presentation is discrepant for a missing
  required document — regardless of how well the other documents check.
- **Draft tenor** — field 42C of the credit fixes the tenor of the
  draft (e.g. "at sight", "at 30 days sight", "at 60 days after BL
  date"). A draft / covering schedule presenting a different tenor is
  a data conflict under UCP 14(d) and is therefore a discrepancy. The
  named tenor must match the credit *exactly*.

## Typical Discrepancy Decision Framework

For each line item where data in one document differs from data in
another document or from the credit:

1. **Identify the field and the conflict.**
2. **Locate the governing rule** (UCP 600 article / ISBP 821 paragraph).
3. **Classify**:
   - **Material discrepancy** — issuing bank may refuse under Art 16.
     Examples: late shipment, late presentation, classification change
     in goods description, BL signed by unidentified agent, weight
     conflict between docs, insufficient insurance coverage, missing
     required document, missing on-board notation.
   - **Non-material** — within tolerance, allowed by rule, or
     typographical and not affecting meaning. Examples: amount short
     within Art 30(c) tolerance, case differences in names, insurance
     at exactly 110%, generic description on non-invoice doc that does
     not conflict.
4. **Final decision**: if any material discrepancy exists → presentation
   is discrepant → either refuse under Art 16, request applicant
   waiver, or request amendment from beneficiary. If only non-material
   items exist → presentation is compliant and must be honoured.

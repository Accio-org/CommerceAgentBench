## 2026-11-24 — Sales Director (Liu Wei)

Quick framing for the week. Inbound queue is heavy this week (5 new
RFQs across LATAM, EMEA, MENA and UK) and we have one factory line
freeing up in mid-January. Priority is to give clean responses
quickly — both supportive and corrective where buyers have
unrealistic expectations. We'd rather lose a few unwinnable leads
than ship a quote that bakes in a problem we have to absorb later.

Standing rule: every quote that goes out must read like the buyer is
also a partner. If the buyer's RFQ has an internal inconsistency
(impossible transit, conflicting Incoterm, etc.), we flag it
politely in the response and offer an alternative — not silently
quote the impossible thing and hope. Our reputation is built on
catching those before the contract.

## 2026-11-25 — Compliance Reviewer (Ekaterina Volkova)

A few notes that apply to multiple RFQs this week:

- INMETRO certification is a real and expensive obligation for goods
  sold at retail in Brazil. Suppliers without an existing INMETRO
  test plan typically add several weeks and ~$0.08-0.12 per unit to
  small-cell-phone-accessory cost. Do not promise INMETRO without
  confirming with the certification team.
- FDA food-contact "compliance" is a self-certified standard in
  practice; we have it for our food-grade SS304 stainless line. The
  buyer language sometimes asks for "FDA approval" which is not a
  thing — clarify politely in the reply.
- BSCI compliance from our two Zhejiang factories is current
  through end of 2027 — Tesco-line buyers should accept that
  certification.
- Lithium-battery products require IMDG / IATA dangerous-goods
  documentation for any air freight. None of the products in this
  week's queue look dangerous-goods, but flag if you see one.
